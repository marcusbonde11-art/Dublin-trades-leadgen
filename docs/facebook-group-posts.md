## Before you post (group norms)

I couldn't read the rules for these three groups. Facebook group pages and
their rules require a login, and web search didn't turn up anything about
these specific groups. So I've treated them the way groups like these usually
work:

| Group | Likely stance on promotion | Version to use |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC-Electrical trade group | Strictest. Trade groups are for people in the trade, and vendors pitching get removed fast. | **B**: a question only, plus an honest line about who you are |
| Columbus Ohio Contractors | Medium. Business talk is fine, ads usually aren't. | **A**: pain points first, a one-paragraph intro, then a question |
| Small Businesses In Columbus Ohio | Loosest. These groups often welcome "introduce yourself" posts. | **C**: an intro with a light offer |

Checklist for each group:

- [ ] Open the group's **About → Group rules** and read them all. Look for
      "no self-promotion", "vendors must get admin approval" or "promo
      Fridays only".
- [ ] If the rules say promotion is allowed only in a weekly thread or with
      admin approval, do that. Messaging the admin first ("Mind if I post a
      question for the owners in here? I'm not selling anything in the
      post") goes a long way.
- [ ] Don't post the same text in all three groups on the same day. Facebook
      flags copy-paste posts as spam, and members who are in more than one
      group will notice. Space them out by a few days.
- [ ] If you can, comment helpfully on a few other posts in the group first.
- [ ] Post on a weekday morning or evening, not mid-day. Owners are on jobs then.
- [ ] Check the line marked **[check]**. Keep it only if it's true for you.

## Version B: HVAC-Electrical trade group (strict, question only)

> Honest question for the shop owners here. I'm not in the trade myself, so I'm asking to learn.
>
> When you're on a job and can't pick up, what happens to the calls? Voicemail, an answering service, someone in the office, or do you just call them back at lunch?
>
> And how many of those people do you think have already called someone else by the time you get back to them?
>
> For context, I'm Marcus from Dublin. I work on the office and phone side of small trade businesses (missed calls, scheduling, reminders), so I'm trying to understand how you all actually handle it instead of guessing. I'm not selling anything here. I'd just like to hear how you do it.

Why this one is different: trade groups tend to tolerate questions from
outsiders but not vendor posts. It names no service and makes no offer, but it
still says plainly who you are. Leaving that out and having someone find your
business page later would hurt you more than the post could help. If people
ask what you do, answer briefly in the comments and offer to take it to
messages.

## When people reply

- **Answer in the comments first, and keep it there.** Ask a follow-up
  question ("How many calls a week do you think go to voicemail?") before
  offering anything.
- **Move to messages only when they show interest** ("How would that work for
  me?") or ask for it. Don't cold-message everyone who liked the post.
- **No links in the first reply.** Many groups auto-hold comments that
  contain links.
- If a trade owner gives a real answer, it's fine to thank them and say
  "That's exactly what I help with. If you ever want to talk it through, my
  inbox is open." Say it once, then leave it.
- If an admin removes the post, message them, apologize, and ask what would
  be acceptable. Don't repost.
- Add anyone who turns into a real conversation to the Pipeline sheet by
  hand, with the source "Facebook: <group>", so they don't also get a cold
  email later.
